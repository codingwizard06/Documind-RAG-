INCIDENT RESPONSE RUNBOOK

1. SEVERITY LEVELS
- SEV1 (Critical): Complete service outage or data loss affecting all customers.
  Response time: immediate, all-hands. Must be acknowledged within 5 minutes.
- SEV2 (High): Major functionality degraded for a significant subset of customers.
  Response time: acknowledge within 15 minutes, resolve within 4 hours.
- SEV3 (Medium): Minor functionality issue with a workaround available.
  Response time: acknowledge within 1 hour, resolve within 1 business day.
- SEV4 (Low): Cosmetic or non-urgent issue. Response time: next sprint cycle.

2. ON-CALL ESCALATION
The primary on-call engineer is paged first via PagerDuty. If there is no
acknowledgment within 5 minutes, the page automatically escalates to the secondary
on-call engineer, then to the engineering manager after another 5 minutes. For SEV1
incidents, the incident commander role must be assigned within 10 minutes of
detection.

3. INCIDENT COMMANDER RESPONSIBILITIES
The incident commander coordinates the response, makes final calls on mitigation
strategy, manages communication with stakeholders, and ensures a scribe is
documenting the timeline. The incident commander does not personally debug the
issue; that responsibility belongs to the assigned technical lead.

4. COMMUNICATION PROTOCOL
For SEV1 and SEV2 incidents, a status page update must be posted within 15 minutes
of confirmation. Internal stakeholders are updated every 30 minutes via the
#incidents Slack channel. Customer-facing communication is drafted by the incident
commander and approved by the on-call communications lead before posting.

5. ROLLBACK PROCEDURE
If a recent deployment is suspected as the root cause, the on-call engineer should
initiate a rollback using the deployment pipeline's one-click revert. Rollbacks do
not require additional approval during an active SEV1 or SEV2 incident but must be
logged in the incident ticket immediately afterward.

6. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW
A blameless post-incident review must be scheduled within 5 business days of
resolution for all SEV1 and SEV2 incidents. The review document includes a timeline,
root cause analysis using the five-whys method, contributing factors, and at least
three concrete action items with owners and due dates.

7. DATA LOSS INCIDENTS
Any incident involving potential data loss or corruption must involve the database
reliability team immediately, regardless of severity classification. Backups are
retained with point-in-time recovery for the last 35 days; recovery procedures are
documented separately in the disaster recovery runbook.

8. MONITORING AND ALERTING THRESHOLDS
Alerts trigger when error rates exceed 1% over a 5-minute rolling window, p99 latency
exceeds 2 seconds, or CPU utilization on any production node exceeds 85% for more
than 10 minutes. Alert fatigue is reviewed monthly, and thresholds are tuned based on
false positive rates.
